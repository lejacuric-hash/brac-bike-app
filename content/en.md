# The Lost Chronicles: Loda's Alphabet

## The world of the game

### The story

Loda the shepherd, the faun of Brač from Vladimir Nazor's novel, has always been on the island. He has seen cave hunters, Illyrians, Romans, Glagolitic priests, princes, sculptors and tourists in cycling helmets. It annoys him when people say Brač is "an island without history".

Loda claims that when he and young Vladimir Nazor sat together in a boat, he whispered to the boy the words of a poem. Nazor later wrote them down in the prose poem *Otok Brač* (The Island of Brač), which he added to his novel *Pastir Loda* (Loda the Shepherd). Loda has hidden the letters of that poem all over the island, each in a place that remembers something. Whoever collects all the letters can put together the sentence in which Nazor thanks his island:

> ***Otoče bez vode, hvala ti, što me nauči žeđati i čeznuti za nečim čitav svoj život.***
>
> *O island without water, thank you for teaching me to thirst and to long for something all my life.*

Nazor died in 1949, so his works have been free to use since 2020. We quote the poem with the author's name. Sources give two forms, *nauči* and *nauče*, so the exact form must be checked in an edition of the novel before printing.

**Loda's voice:** mischievous, warm and a little boastful. Loda is short and bow-legged, with a smile from ear to ear. He loves goats, sheep, olive oil, wine and a good story, and signs his letters "— Loda". The real-life model for the character was the shepherd Antun (Ante) Sapunar, whose memories Nazor wrote down in Ložišća.

### Loda's alphabet

Nazor's sentence has 16 words but only **20 different letters**. That is why the game is built as an alphabet:

- **20 stops, 20 letters.** Each stop gives one Glagolitic letter, and no letter repeats.
- **The order does not matter.** Brač by Bike guests start from different places on the island, so the stops can be visited in any order and in either direction.
- **Words unlock by themselves.** As soon as a player has all the letters of a word, the app unlocks it together with its English translation. Each word also stands on its own.
- **The sentence at the end.** When a player has all 20 letters, they arrange the unlocked words into Nazor's sentence.

The words are Croatian in both editions of the game, shown with an English translation underneath.

| Word | Translation | Letters needed |
|---|---|---|

| BEZ | without | B, E, Z |

| NAUČI | (you) taught | N, A, U, Č, I |

### Five games and three prizes

The sentence is collected in three parts, and each part brings a prize:

| Prize | When the player has | Letters | Which games |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st, small | **OTOČE** | O, T, Č, E | Game 1 |
| 2nd, medium | **OTOČE BEZ VODE, HVALA TI** | + B, Z, V, D, H, A, L, I | Games 1, 2 and 3 |
| 3rd, grand | the whole sentence | + Š, M, N, U, Ž, Đ, S, J | All five games |

The first three games are easy to reach from Supetar, where the main Brač by Bike shop is. The last two lead into the heart of the island and to the south, where the most demanding and most beautiful stops are. Within each prize level the order does not matter, so Games 2 and 3 can be played in either order, and guests staying in Bol can start with Game 5.

| Game | Part of the island | Letters | Length | Difficulty | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Otoče | around Supetar | T, O, Č, E | about 30 km | easy | half a day |
| 2. The West | Ložišća, Bobovišća, Milna, Dračevica | Z, B, L, D | about 50 km | medium | 1 day |
| 3. The North | Kopačina, Škrip, Postira, Dol | A, I, V, H | about 40 km | medium | 1 day |
| 4. The Heart of the Island | Nerežišća, Nerežišća plain, Koloč | Đ, U, N | about 30 km with a climb | medium | 1 day |
| 5. The South | Zlatni Rat, Bol, Dragon's Cave, Blaca, Vidova gora | Ž, J, M, Š, S | about 45 km plus walking | hard | 2 days |

All distances are approximate and must be checked on a map. Besides the stops with a letter, each game has **bonus stories**: places along the way that Loda shows and talks about, but that give no letter. Selca, Pučišća, Povlja and Sumartin form an optional trip to the east, without letters.

### How a stop works

1. **Loda's story.** A short, humorous text that hides a real fact about the place.
2. **Trivia question.** The answer is in Loda's story, in the facts about the place, or visible on site.
3. **A Glagolitic letter.** A correct answer unlocks a letter, shown in Glagolitic. The player decodes it with Loda's key, which they receive at the first stop they visit. Glagolitic is a script created for the Croatian language, and Glagolitic priests lived on Brač, in the Dragon's Cave and at Blaca.
4. **Photo task.** The player photographs a given detail as proof that they were there. Once wooden boards with QR codes are agreed with the tourist boards, scanning the code will replace the photo, and the photo task becomes a bonus. Scanning outside the app leads to the app download page.
5. **Clue.** A sentence that leads to the next stop of the game, in one direction of the loop. The player can also go the other way round.

### Prizes

This is a proposal; Brač by Bike decides the final prizes. Prizes are collected at the shop with a unique code from the app.

| For | Prize |

| 1st prize: OTOČE | Loda's key ring, a wooden tag with a Glagolitic letter |

### Rules for cyclists

- The interior of the island lies at 300–500 m, and Vidova gora at 778 m. E-bikes are recommended for Games 4 and 5.
- Walking sections: the Kopačina plateau, Koloč rock, the Dragon's Cave (with a guide only) and the Blaca Hermitage (footpath or boat from Bol).
- Photo tasks never ask players to climb walls or rocks, enter private yards, pick plants or touch animals.
- Water, a helmet and lights are compulsory. In summer, avoid riding at midday.

## Game 1: Otoče – around Supetar

**Letters:** T, O, Č, E → the word **OTOČE** and the **1st prize**  
**Route:** Supetar → Likva Bay → Sutivan → back to Supetar → Splitska and the Rasohe quarry → back to Supetar  
**Length:** about 30 km along the coast, easy. Plus about 4 km on foot to Rasohe and back. Takes half a day.  
**Best for:** the first day on the island, straight off the ferry or after picking up a bike in Supetar.

**Loda's letter:**

> *Welcome to my island. The first four stops are close together, by the sea, so you won't even have to pedal hard. At the end you'll have the first word, the one the poet used to call out to this island. — Loda*

### Stop: Supetar, Petrinović mausoleum → T

**Loda's story:** *See that white dome on the peninsula? A young man crossed the ocean as a stowaway, with nothing. He came back rich and had a tomb built like nothing the island had ever seen, with five domes and angels of white stone. Rendić, the best sculptor in Supetar, was meant to carve it, but the job was taken from him and given to another sculptor. Stone remembers glory, and it remembers injustice too.*

**Facts:** The Petrinović family mausoleum in the cemetery on the St Nicholas peninsula was built in 1924–1927 of white Brač stone, to a design by the sculptor Toma Rosandić. Frano Petrinović went to Chile as a stowaway and became a shipowner and mine owner there. The commission first went to Ivan Rendić, who considered it his life's work.

**Question:** What was the first name of the sculptor who designed the mausoleum? → **T** (Toma)

**Photo task:** The mausoleum with the sea behind it. This is a cemetery, so take photos quietly and respectfully.

**Bonus stories in Supetar:**

- *The Basilica of St Peter.* Supetar is named after the early Christian basilica of St Peter, whose remains, with part of its floor mosaic, survive beside today's parish church.
- *The well of 1734.* Beside the church stands a stone well with reliefs, the work of master Johannes Mazzonius. On an island without water, rainwater was collected in cisterns, and a well was a treasure.

**Clue:** *Ride west along the sea, to the bay where the sky once fell to Earth.*

### Stop: Likva Bay → O

**Loda's story:** *Sixty-six million years ago, far across the ocean, a rock bigger than this island fell from the sky. The seas rose, and a wave left a thin layer of sand here. That sand hardened into stone. Below it is the world of giant lizards, and above it begins the world in which sheep, people and I would one day be born. Don't touch it. Just look.*

**Facts:** Likva Bay preserves layers from the boundary between the Cretaceous and the Paleogene (the K-Pg boundary), the transition from the age of dinosaurs to the age of mammals. The boundary is marked by a layer of petrified sand about 10 cm thick, deposited by a tsunami caused by an asteroid impact 66 million years ago. In January 2026 the site was declared the Likva Geological Natural Monument.

**Question:** The sand the tsunami left here has turned to stone. What is sand that has turned to stone called? → **O** (petrified)

**Photo task:** The rocks of the Likva headland with the sea in the frame, taken from the path. Do not climb the rocks or break anything off: the site is protected.

**Clue:** *Carry on along the sea to the village that has just put up a monument to everyone who left across the ocean.*

### Stop: Sutivan, emigration monument → Č

**Loda's story:** *A four-year-old boy left this village with his parents and crossed the ocean. He became a doctor in a town on the edge of the desert and spent his whole life treating the poor for free. They still call him the doctor of the poor there. In August 2026 Sutivan put up a monument to him, and to everyone who left. The monument shows an old man and a boy. One leaves, the other remembers.*

**Facts:** Antonio (Ante) Rendić Ivanović was born in Sutivan in 1896 and emigrated with his family in 1899 to Chile, to the town of Antofagasta, where as a doctor he earned the nickname "doctor of the poor". A process for his beatification has been opened. On 4 August 2026 Sutivan unveiled the first monument to emigration from Brač and to him, a work by the academician Kažimir Hraste, in the central park in front of the municipal building.

**Question:** To which country did Dr Rendić Ivanović emigrate? → **Č** (Chile)

**Photo task:** The emigration monument in the central park, with the figures of the old man and the boy.

**Bonus stories in Sutivan:**

- *Kavanjin's summer house.* In the summer house on the waterfront, Jerolim Kavanjin wrote an epic poem of 32,724 lines, the longest work of older Croatian literature.
- *The geological column on the Blato promenade,* opened in 2023 as the first of its kind on the Dalmatian islands, and the fossil ammonites and rudists in the walls of old houses, shown on the information boards in the village centre.

**Clue:** *Ride back through Supetar and head east, to a small harbour. Above it, in the rock, stands a Greek hero my father used to drink with.*

### Stop: Splitska and the Rasohe quarry → E

**Loda's story:** *Here he is, the hero in the rock. The quarrymen carved him to protect them while they cut stone for the emperor's palace. Look at his hands. They're far too big, because anyone who cuts stone knows that strength is in the hands. The quarrymen brought him offerings, and me nothing. And I looked after their flock while they worked. That's justice for you.*

**Facts:** A 2 km themed trail, *Herkules*, leads from the Splitska waterfront to the quarry. The relief of Hercules is about 80 cm high, and the style of the beard dates it to the 3rd century. Hercules wears the skin of the Nemean lion and holds the apples of the Hesperides and a club. Three Roman quarries lie between Splitska and Škrip, Plate, Stražišće and Rasohe, where stone was cut for Diocletian's Palace.

**Question:** Which hero is carved into the rock at the entrance to the quarry? → **E** (Hercules)

**Photo task:** Hercules in full, so that both oversized hands are visible. Do not touch the relief.

**Word:** The letters T, O, Č, E unlock **OTOČE**.  
*English: O island*

**Loda's message:** *Otoče! O island! That is how the poet spoke to this stone, like calling a friend. The first word is yours, and with it the first prize. Come to the shop.*

## Game 2: The West – villages, poets and ships

**Letters:** Z, B, L, D  
**Loop:** Supetar → Sutivan → Ložišća → Bobovišća na Moru → Milna → Dračevica → Donji Humac → Supetar  
**Length:** about 50 km, medium, with climbs from the coast up to Ložišća and to Dračevica.  
**Best for:** guests in Supetar, Sutivan and Milna.

**Loda's letter:**

> *The west of the island is gentler than the rest: soft bays, pines down to the sea and hill villages looking out at Šolta. This is where I met the boy who later put me in a book. Today I'll show you his villages. — Loda*

### Stop: Ložišća → Z

**Loda's story:** *When the people of Ložišća asked for a design, the sculptor Rendić showed them, as a joke, a lavish one meant for Venice. "You can neither build this nor pay for it," he told them. And they said: "This one and no other!" They borrowed the money and paid the debt back in wine and olive oil. An old shepherd, Ante, used to come to this village with his sheep. Little Vladimir listened to his stories. That's how I got into the book.*

**Facts:** The monumental bell tower of the parish church of Saints John and Paul was built in 1889 to a design by the sculptor Ivan Rendić. The story of the "bell tower for Venice" was written down by the local barber and postman, Frane Pavlov. Ložišća was called Velo selo (the Big Village) and Bobovišća Malo selo (the Little Village). Nazor's family moved to Ložišća when he was five, and the shepherd Antun Sapunar, the model for Loda the shepherd, used to visit the village.

**Question:** What did Rendić design for the people of Ložišća "for Venice"? → **Z** (bell tower)

**Photo task:** The bell tower from the foot of the stone steps, so that it fits in whole, up to the top.

**Clue:** *Ride down to the bay that splits in two. One arm has boats, the other old warriors' graves.*

### Stop: Bobovišća na Moru, Vičja Luka → B

**Loda's story:** *Warriors lay in these graves with helmets and greaves, two hundred years before anyone had heard of Rome. Their armour was made of a metal that shines like gold when you polish it. The old people say that at night the štringe, the witches of Brač, gather in Vičja Luka. It's true, I've danced with them. They dance badly.*

**Facts:** Finds from the 5th and 4th centuries BC were discovered in the graves above Vičja Luka: coins, bronze helmets and greaves from Corinthian workshops. The bay is dominated by the fortified castle of the Gligo family, with stone rings for mooring boats. Legend says that witches gather in Vičja Luka.

**Question:** What metal were the warriors' helmets found in the graves made of? → **B** (bronze)

**Photo task:** A stone mooring ring on the wall of the Gligo castle, with the sea in the frame.

**Bonus story:** *The Three Sisters.* Nazor's family had a house here, and he went to school here. In 1937 he had three columns put up on the hill above the bathing area in honour of his sisters.

**Clue:** *Ride west to the busiest old harbour on the island, where the vineyards once sent half the village across the ocean.*

### Stop: Milna → L

**Loda's story:** *When disease destroyed the vineyards of Italy and France, the people of Milna planted new ones on every scrap of stone. For a few years things went well. Then the disease came here too, and half the families left for America. Those who stayed turned to the sea. The fishermen threw the first fish they caught onto the shore for the macići, little dwarfs in red caps.*

**Facts:** From the 17th to the 19th century Milna was the busiest harbour on Brač. When vine disease devastated Italian and French vineyards, the people of Milna cleared land and planted new vineyards, as the stone heaps and dry-stone walls still show. The disease soon spread here too, and half of Milna's families emigrated to America. In 1806 the French built the Baterija fort on Cape Zaglav.

**Question:** Which plant did the people of Milna plant on every scrap of stone? → **L** (vine)

**Photo task:** Milna harbour with the church of Our Lady of the Annunciation, and the hills with dry-stone walls behind.

**Bonus story:** *Macići.* According to a legend kept by the Milna Tourist Board, fishermen believed in macići, dwarfs in red caps, and asked them where to cast their nets. They threw the first fish they caught onto the shore, saying: "Here you are, mace, your lunch."

**Clue:** *Climb inland to the village of refugees, named after thorns.*

### Stop: Dračevica → D

**Loda's story:** *Refugees from Poljica came here in 1574 with empty hands. Stone and thorns were waiting for them. They cleared the land for years, planted vines and olives and grew rich. On the hill they built the biggest windmill on the island, and it ground grain for everyone. The village's name, they say, comes from the thorns they cleared. My flock loved them and I hated them, because they always got tangled in my beard.*

**Facts:** Dračevica was founded by refugees from Poljica who settled on Brač in 1574. Its name most probably comes from *drača*, the thorny shrub (Christ's thorn) common on the karst. Above the village stand the walls of the biggest windmill on Brač, which ground grain for the whole island. The linguist and academician Petar Šimunović, author of *Brač Place Names*, was born here.

**Question:** Which thorny shrub is Dračevica probably named after? Use its local name. → **D** (drača)

**Photo task:** The walls of the windmill on the hilltop above the village, or a thorny shrub beside a dry-stone wall.

**Clue for the ride back:** *Ride through Donji Humac back to Supetar.*

**Loda's message:** *Four letters from the west. Together with the letters from Games 1 and 3, you are close to half the sentence.*

## Game 3: The North – the first people, stone and the poet

**Letters:** A, I, V, H  
**Loop:** Supetar → Donji Humac (Kopačina plateau) → Škrip → Splitska → Postira → Dol → Postira → Supetar  
**Length:** about 40 km, medium, with climbs to Donji Humac, Škrip and Dol. Plus a short walk to Kopačina. The road link between Donji Humac and Škrip must be checked on a map.  
**Best for:** guests in Supetar, Splitska and Postira.

**Loda's letter:**

> *The north of the island keeps the oldest traces of people and the oldest village. The man who wrote me down was born here too. When you have these letters, together with those from the first two games, half the sentence will be yours. — Loda*

### Stop: Kopačina Cave plateau near Donji Humac → A

**Loda's story:** *Before the Illyrians, before the hillforts, before any walls at all, the first people on Brač lived here. They hunted wild horses, cattle and deer, and made knives and arrowheads out of stone. Back then Brač was not yet an island: there was no sea, only a valley. When they lived here, I wasn't even an idea.*

**Facts:** Kopačina is the most interesting prehistoric site on the island, with the oldest traces of people on Brač: bones of wild horse, wild cattle and deer, and stone knives, drills and an arrowhead. It is about a 20-minute walk from Donji Humac; the path turns off by the cemetery. The entrance is closed with a grille because of archaeological excavations, so visitors only go to the plateau in front of the cave. Some of the finds are on display at the Brač Island Museum in Škrip.

**Question:** Which science studies the remains of the first people at Kopačina by excavation? → **A** (archaeology)

**Photo task:** The view from the plateau down the valley towards the sea. Do not enter the cave.

**Bonus story:** *Donji Humac, a village of stonemasons and cherries.* People here live from working stone, and grow cherries alongside vines and olives. At sculpture symposia, sculptors left stone sculptures in Donji Humac, Nerežišća and Dračevica.

**Clue:** *Ride east to the oldest village on the island, where the walls are built of huge blocks without mortar.*

### Stop: Škrip → I

**Loda's story:** *This is the oldest village on the island. The hillfort people built these walls from blocks of stone so big that later everyone thought giants had laid them. They didn't. People laid them, without mortar, and the walls still stand. The Romans put a tomb on top of them, and later someone put a tower on the tomb. On this island, people always build on top of something older.*

**Facts:** Škrip is the oldest settlement on Brač. Part of the Illyrian walls can be seen in the courtyard of the Brač Island Museum, in the Radojković tower, which stands on the foundations of a Roman mausoleum. The name Škrip probably comes from the Latin *scrupus*, sharp stones.

**Question:** Which people built the walls in Škrip? → **I** (Illyrians)

**Photo task:** The Illyrian walls in the museum courtyard, with the tower also in the frame.

**Bonus stories in Škrip:**

- *The Olive Oil Museum.* A family oil mill that worked until 1963 keeps its old presses. Since 2022, Brač olive oil has had a protected designation of origin in the EU, and must contain at least 80% of the Oblica variety.
- *Legends of Škrip.* Legend says that Diocletian's wife Prisca and daughter Valeria are buried in the Roman mausoleum, that St Helena was born in Škrip, and that the Trojan hero Antenor brought to the island the shepherd Brah, in whom Loda can be recognised.

**Clue:** *Ride down to the coast and east, to the village whose name means pasture. The man who wrote me down was born there.*

### Stop: Postira → V

**Loda's story:** *In 1876 a boy was born here who later found me. He went far away, among Greek temples and Egyptian pyramids, and there he began to long for this island. When he came back, he sat with me in a boat and listened to my stories. Then he wrote a book about me, and at the end of it he put the poem your letters come from.*

**Facts:** Vladimir Nazor was born in Postira on 30 May 1876, and his birth house is a protected monument. On a journey around the Mediterranean he began to long for the island of his childhood, and from that longing came the novel *Pastir Loda*, with the prose poem *Otok Brač* as its epilogue.

**Question:** What was the first name of the author of *Pastir Loda*, born in Postira? → **V** (Vladimir)

**Photo task:** The outside of Nazor's birth house.

**Bonus story:** *Capris laudata Brattia.* The name Postira probably comes from the Latin *pastura*, pasture. The Roman writer Pliny the Elder wrote that Brač was famous for its goats. Loda considers this the greatest compliment in history.

**Clue:** *Climb to the village at the end of the valley, where the houses, the walls and even a cake are made of the same red stone.*

### Stop: Dol → H

**Loda's story:** *The stone here isn't white. It's red and rough, as if someone had glued together a thousand chips of rock. People built their houses from it, and the women of Dol named a cake after it, because it looks the same: rough, reddish and full of walnuts. They brought it as a gift to weddings all over the island. I came to every one. Nobody invited me, but I always got cake.*

**Facts:** Dol has some thirty shallow caves in a reddish, grainy stone called hrapoćuša, a breccia, from which the houses and dry-stone walls are also built. The Dol cake hrapoćuša is named after this stone; its preparation was listed in 2012 as a protected intangible cultural heritage of Croatia. Every August the village holds the Night of Hrapoćuša.

**Question:** What is the red stone of Dol, which the cake is also named after, called? → **H** (hrapoćuša)

**Photo task:** A wall or cave of hrapoćuša stone, close up. If you buy a piece of the cake, a second shot is the cake next to the wall, for comparison.

**Bonus story:** *The viver and the owls.* Legend says that the viver, a night creature that hoots, lives in the caves of Dol. Pigeons and owls really do nest in the caves. Stone beehives set into dry-stone walls also survive on the slopes above the village.

**Clue for the ride back:** *Ride down to Postira and along the coast back to Supetar.*

**Loda's message:** *If you've finished the first two games too, look in the app: OTOČE BEZ VODE, HVALA TI. O island without water, thank you. Half the sentence is yours, and with it the second prize.*

## Game 4: The Heart of the Island – the prince, the fields and the stone arch

**Letters:** Đ, U, N  
**Loop:** Supetar → Donji Humac → Nerežišća → Nerežišća plain → Koloč rock → back to Supetar  
**Length:** about 30 km, medium, with a climb from the sea to the plateau at 300–400 m. Plus about 30 minutes on foot to Koloč.  
**Best for:** guests in Supetar and anyone who wants to see the interior of the island, where there are hardly any tourists.

**Loda's letter:**

> *The tourists are on the coast, and the shepherds are in the heart of the island. For eight centuries the prince sat here, farmers laid stones into walls by hand to win a scrap of soil, and water carved a cake out of the rock. This is my home. Welcome. — Loda*

### Stop: Nerežišća → Đ

**Loda's story:** *For eight centuries the prince sat here. He settled quarrels over boundaries and flocks, and every year he asked for more tax. The town was built inland because pirates came from the sea. Look at the fields around the village. Every piece of land was won from the stone, and the stones were laid into walls between the fields. On Brač they call them meje.*

**Facts:** Nerežišća was the seat of government of the island and of the Prince of Brač from around the year 1000 until the Napoleonic era. It lies on the edge of the largest plain on Brač, and people still live from farming and livestock, especially the famous Brač lamb. The dry-stone walls that divide the plots are called meje on Brač; the standard Croatian word is međa, a boundary.

**Question:** What are the dry-stone walls between the fields called on Brač? → **Đ** (meje)

**Photo task:** A dry-stone wall between two fields on the edge of the village. Do not climb the walls.

**Bonus stories in Nerežišća:**

- *The pine on the roof.* A black pine about 170 cm tall and 150 to 200 years old grows out of the roof of the small church of Saints Peter and Paul. It is protected as a natural monument and known as the "Brač bonsai".
- *Brač vitalac.* A shepherds' dish of lamb offal on a spit, associated with Nerežišća, is a protected intangible cultural heritage of Croatia.

**Clue:** *Go out into the fields. Between them lie mounds of stones that nobody piled up for a flock.*

### Stop: Nerežišća plain, prehistoric mounds → U

**Loda's story:** *Some of the stone heaps in the fields were piled up by farmers clearing the land. But some are much older. Thousands of years ago people buried their dead under them. As a boy, the poet Vladimir thought they were temples, raised by the work of many hands. He wasn't far from the truth.*

**Facts:** The Nerežišća plain holds prehistoric stone burial mounds, known locally as gomile. The scientific name for such a mound is a tumulus. Nazor remembered the stone heaps of Brač as a kind of temple, raised by the patient work of many hands.

**Question:** What is the scientific name for a prehistoric burial mound? → **U** (tumulus)

**Photo task:** A mound in the field, photographed from the path. Do not climb the mounds or take stones from them.

**Clue:** *Head south-west past the little church of St James, to the fertile hollow where two stone giants are kissing.*

### Stop: Koloč rock → N

**Loda's story:** *Nobody carved this. Water, roots and time did the work. They carried away the softer stone, and the harder stone was left standing in a ring. The old people named it after a festive cake. Romantics say two stone giants are kissing here. I knew them. They weren't kissing, they were arguing about a sheep.*

**Facts:** Koloč (Kolač) is a natural stone arch about 12 m high, a ring-shaped rock of limestone and dolomite, south-west of Nerežišća. It was formed by erosion: nature and plant roots wore away the softer parts of the rock, and the harder parts were left in the shape of a ring. It has been protected as a geomorphological natural monument since 1986. From the end of the road it is about a 30-minute walk on flat ground, and there is an information board at the site.

**Question:** Which village is Koloč rock near? → **N** (Nerežišća)

**Photo task:** The sky photographed through the opening of Koloč.

**Clue for the ride back:** *Go back through Nerežišća and down via Donji Humac to Supetar. If you're carrying on south, climb from here towards Vidova gora, where Game 5 begins.*

**Loda's message:** *Three letters from the heart of the island. Only the south is left before the whole sentence.*

## An optional trip to the east (no letters)

For guests with time to spare or staying in the east of the island. These places give no letters, but Loda has stories to tell. They are easiest to combine with Game 5, from Bol via Gornji Humac, or as a separate day from Postira along the coast.

- **Pučišća.** A town of towers, stone and stonemasons. Because of its houses fortified against pirates it is also called the harbour of towers, and at the stonemasonry school pupils still learn to work Brač stone by hand. Brač people call a master stonemason a *meštar*. It is often claimed that Brač stone was built into the White House in Washington, but the evidence is not conclusive.
- **Selca.** In 1914 pupils of the Selca stonemasonry school carved a bust of Leo Tolstoy from a model by the Czech sculptor Jaroslav Barda. It is considered the first monument to Tolstoy in the world. The idea came from the Slovak doctor and writer Matej Bencúr.
- **Povlja.** The Povlja lintel from around 1184 mentions a stonemason, Master Radonja. The Povlja Charter of 1250 is the oldest surviving document from Brač, the "birth certificate of the island".
- **Sumartin.** It was founded in the mid-17th century by Franciscans and refugees from the Makarska coast fleeing the Ottomans. The monastery is linked to the poet Andrija Kačić Miošić.

## Game 5: The South – the mountain, the dragon and the stars

**Letters:** Ž, J, M, Š, S → with them, the whole sentence and the 3rd, grand prize  
**Loop:** Bol → Zlatni Rat → Dominican monastery → Murvica and the Dragon's Cave → Blaca Hermitage → Vidova gora → down to Bol  
**Length:** about 45 km by bike, plus walking to the Dragon's Cave and Blaca. A hard route, best over two days with Bol as a base.  
**Suggested days:** Day 1: Bol, Zlatni Rat, the Dominicans, Murvica and the Dragon's Cave. Day 2: Blaca by boat from Bol and the climb up Vidova gora. Guests coming from Supetar or Nerežišća can start at Vidova gora and ride down to Bol.  
**Best for:** guests in Bol and Murvica, and anyone who wants the finest views on the island.

**Loda's letter:**

> *The south of the island is steep and wild. The mountain drops straight into the sea, and in the rocks hid priests who wrote in old letters, carved dragons and watched the stars. Everything here is a little closer to the sky. Bring water, good shoes and brakes you've checked. — Loda*

### Stop: Zlatni Rat → Ž

**Loda's story:** *This cape is never the same. Torrents brought the pebbles down from the mountain, and the sea and the wind keep arranging and rearranging them, so the tip points east one day and west the next. Stand on the tip and listen. That sound is stone that isn't finished yet. And from the pine wood behind you, the cicadas are splitting your ears. There are about twenty kinds of them on this island.*

**Facts:** Zlatni Rat is made of pebbles that torrents brought down from Vidova gora. It changes shape with the winds and sea currents, and has been protected as a significant landscape since 1965. The old pines in the protected area still show scars from resin tapping, a craft that has died out. It is best known for the maestral, the summer north-westerly wind that draws windsurfers.

**Question:** Zlatni Rat is a beach made of pebbles. What is such a pebble or sand shore called in Croatian? → **Ž** (žal)

**Photo task:** A handful of pebbles in your palm, with the tip of the cape behind. The pebbles stay on the beach.

**Clue:** *Go back through Bol to its eastern cape, where monks keep a painting by a great master.*

### Stop: Bol, Dominican monastery → J

**Loda's story:** *In 1475 the people of Bol gave the monks land on this cape, and the Prince of Brač confirmed the gift with a charter that is still kept today. The monks collected books, old coins and paintings. One painting is attributed to a great master from Venice. I've been to Venice. Everybody paints there, but only a few are masters.*

**Facts:** The monastery of St Mary of Mercy was founded in 1475, and the original deed of gift from the Prince of Brač, granting the monks the land, survives. The monastery has a museum, archaeological finds and a library of rare books and manuscripts. The altarpiece of the Virgin and Child is attributed to the Venetian painter Jacopo Tintoretto.

**Question:** What was the first name of Tintoretto, to whom the altarpiece is attributed? → **J** (Jacopo)

**Photo task:** The monastery and church with the sea, taken from the Bol waterfront.

**Bonus story:** *Bol's plavac.* Plavac, the well-known red wine of Brač, is grown on the steep southern slopes around Bol. An old legend says the vine was brought to Brač by the vine grower Silen, who arrived together with the shepherd Brah, that is, Loda.

**Clue:** *Ride west to the village of Murvica. Above it, half an hour's climb, a dragon sleeps behind iron gates.*

### Stop: The Dragon's Cave (Drakonjina špilja) above Murvica → M

**Loda's story:** *In the 15th century, priests who wrote in their own old letters, the ones you're collecting, took refuge here. Into the rock they carved Our Lady with angels, people, birds in nests, a dragon, and something that shines in the sky at night. Some say certain signs are older, left by people who prayed to other gods here. Look at the dragon. It isn't guarding gold. It's watching. On this island even dragons gaze into the distance.*

**Facts:** The cave lies about 180 m above Murvica, is about twenty metres long and divided into four chambers. In the 15th century it was the home and chapel of Glagolitic priests from Poljica. Besides the dragon, the rock carvings show Our Lady with angels, the moon, people and birds in nests, and some experts see traces of pagan symbolism in them. The entrance is locked, and visits are possible only with a local guide. The climb from Murvica takes 30 to 60 minutes.

**Question:** Besides the dragon, Our Lady and the angels, which heavenly body is carved into the rock? → **M** (moon)

**Photo task:** The dragon relief, if the guide allows photos. If not, the view from the cave towards Hvar.

**Clue:** *The same people who lived with the dragon later came down into a hidden valley. There they looked even further, at the stars. The easiest way there is by boat from Bol.*

### Stop: Blaca Hermitage → Š

**Loda's story:** *In 1551 two priests fleeing the Turks hid in a cave in this valley. They stayed for four hundred years. They planted olives, printed books, taught children and kept bees. The last of them, Don Niko, bought the biggest telescope on Croatian soil in Vienna and watched the sky at night. Sometimes I sat next to him. He showed me the planets. I showed him where the best grass for goats was. We were both happy.*

**Facts:** The hermitage was founded in 1551 by Glagolitic priests from Poljica, in the Ljubitovica cave. It became a monastery, a farm, a school, a printing house and an observatory. Its last head, Don Nikola Miličević the Younger, bought a 178 mm refracting telescope in Vienna in 1926, then the largest telescope on Croatian soil. The hermitage ended with his death in 1963. Two asteroids discovered at the Višnjan Observatory are named Nikola Milicevic and Brac. Since 2007 Blaca has been on UNESCO's tentative list of World Heritage sites.

**Question:** Where did the two priests who founded the Blaca Hermitage hide in 1551? → **Š** (a cave)

**Photo task:** The monastery built into the rock, taken from the path above it.

**Clue:** *Finally, climb as high as the island allows, to the peak named after a young saint.*

### Stop: Vidova gora → S

**Loda's story:** *When the Slavs came here, they say they gave the peak to a god with four faces, who looks out on every side of the world. Later a little church to a young saint was built on the top, and the god withdrew. Look at the forest around you. These black pines only grow this high up, so other islands hardly have any. And down below, in the sea, you can see Zlatni Rat, where you started.*

**Facts:** Vidova gora (778 m) is the highest peak of all the Adriatic islands and is protected as a significant landscape of about 1,880 hectares, covered with native Dalmatian black pine. It is named after the church of St Vitus (Vid) from the 13th or 14th century, whose ruins can be seen west of the viewpoint. The theory that the peak was dedicated to the old Slavic god Svetovid has no material evidence.

**Question:** According to the theory, to which four-faced old Slavic god was the peak dedicated? → **S** (Svetovid)

**Photo task:** The view of Zlatni Rat from above, and a second shot of the ruins of the church of St Vitus.

**Bonus stories along the way:**

- *The wild pear near Gornji Humac,* on the plateau, was protected as a natural monument in 1954. It is about 150 years old, and the swallowtail butterfly is often seen near it.
- *Vičja jama.* Legend says the pit in the forest below the peak is linked underground to Vičja Luka near Bobovišća, at the other end of the island.

**Clue for the ride back:** *Take the long hairpins down to Bol. Check your brakes.*

**Loda's message:** *From pebbles to the stars. The south gave you the hardest letters, but also the finest views. If you have all twenty, the whole sentence is yours. Put it together, then come for the grand prize.*

## Finale: Loda's sentence

When the player has all 20 letters, all the words are already unlocked. The app shows them shuffled, and the player puts them in order:

> ___ ___ ___, ___, ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ ___.

Anyone who doesn't know the order can open Loda's hint: the first word calls out to the island, and the last is what a person lives. When everything is in place, the whole sentence opens with its translation:

> ***Otoče bez vode, hvala ti, što me nauči žeđati i čeznuti za nečim čitav svoj život.***
>
> *O island without water, thank you for teaching me to thirst and to long for something all my life.*

**Three prizes along the way:** the app announces the first prize as soon as the player puts together OTOČE, the second when they put together OTOČE BEZ VODE, HVALA TI, and the third, grand prize when they put together the whole sentence. Each prize has its own unique code for the shop.

**Final place (optional):** Vladimir Nazor's birth house in Postira, for a group photo. Prizes are collected at the Brač by Bike shop in Supetar.

**Loda's last letter:**

> *There it is, whole. I whispered those words to little Vladimir while we sat in the boat, or maybe he read them to me. I no longer remember who taught whom. People say Loda was made from an old shepherd, Ante from Ložišća. Maybe I am that Ante. Maybe I am a faun. And maybe I am every shepherd who ever kept goats on this stone and told children about the old days. It doesn't matter. What matters is that you came and did it all by bike. An island without history? Now you know better. Tell someone what you saw. And come back. — Loda*

## Organiser key

### Answers and letters

See `answers.md` for the full list of accepted answers in both languages. The app compares answers regardless of capital letters and diacritics. "With a note" means the app accepts the answer but politely explains which form was needed.

### What is Nazor, what is history, and what is our game

- **Nazor:** the faun Brah, later Loda the shepherd, and the prose poem *Otok Brač*, the source of the sentence. His works have been in the public domain since 2020.
- **Real:** the shepherd Antun (Ante) Sapunar, the model for Loda. Master Radonja, the stonemason named on the Povlja lintel. The folk legend of Antenor, who brought the shepherd Brah, the sailor Elaph and the vine grower Silen to Brač.
- **Our game:** all of Loda's letters and jokes were invented for the game, in the spirit of Nazor's character.
- **Glagolitic:** Croatian words written in Glagolitic make historical sense, because Glagolitic was created for the Croatian language. It has its own letters for č, š, ž and đ, so every letter of the sentence can be written without adaptation.
