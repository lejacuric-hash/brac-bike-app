# The Lost Chronicle of Brač

## The story world

Four cycling games tell one story. The three short games are three chapters, and the multi-day game reveals who wrote the chronicle. The goal is simple: at every stop, the player should stop, look closely, ask a question and want to come back.

*Note for organisers: this is the English edition of the Croatian game. The letter puzzles are rewritten so every answer is an English word; the stories and facts are the same.*

### The story in one sentence

For centuries, someone has been leaving letters in the stone of Brač, signed only with the letter **R.**, and each letter leads to a place where the island still remembers something.

### Who is R.?

The player finds out only at the end of the big game, in Povlja. On the Povlja lintel, one of the oldest inscriptions in the Croatian language (around 1184), a stonemason is named beside Prince Brečko: **Master Radonja**. He was a real person, and we know almost nothing else about him. In our game he is an immortal stonemason who has lived through every age of the island. He cut stone for Diocletian, carved a dragon with the hermit-priests and carried blocks for a village bell tower. The idea is borrowed from *Pastir Loda* (Loda the Shepherd), a novel by Vladimir Nazor about a being who lives on Brač for thousands of years. We use only the idea, not Nazor's text.

### How each stop works

1.  **A letter from R.** A short, atmospheric text read on the spot. It always hides one real historical fact.

2.  **Photo task.** A precisely described shot that proves the player was there. Each shot is chosen to make the player notice a detail they would otherwise miss.

3.  **Letter of the code.** Each stop gives one letter, and the letters form the game's secret word. The letter comes from a question whose answer is in the stop's letter or history text, or can be seen on site, so the game always works.

4.  **True or legend?** One claim per stop. The player votes; the answer is in the Organiser key.

5.  **Clue.** One sentence that points to the next stop.

### Secret words

| Game                           | Secret word         | Meaning                                                           |
|--------------------------------|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Stone for an Emperor       | STONE               | What the island is made of                                        |
| 2\. The Prince's Seal          | BRATTIA             | The Roman name of Brač                                            |
| 3\. The Dragon's Eye           | ASTRA               | Latin for "stars": the summit, the view and the Blaca observatory |
| 4\. The Shepherd Who Remembers | ASPERA              | Latin for "hardships, thorns": the journey around the island      |
| Finale                         | PER ASPERA AD ASTRA | "Through hardship to the stars", the final password               |

The final password is the Latin saying **PER ASPERA AD ASTRA**, "through hardship to the stars" (literally "through thorns"). It is the same in the Croatian and English editions. Players earn ASTRA in Game 3, which ends at the Blaca observatory. They collect ASPERA during the big game, including one letter in Dračevica, a village probably named after *drača*, a thorny shrub. In Povlja, Radonja hands the player the frame of the sentence, *Per ... ad ...*, and at the finale in Škrip the player fills in the two words they have earned.

### Rules for cyclists

- All distances in this document are approximate and must be checked on a map before the game is published. Brač is hilly: the interior lies at 300–500 m and Vidova gora rises to 778 m.

- **E-bikes** are recommended for Games 2, 3 and 4. Game 1 can be ridden on a regular bike by anyone reasonably fit.

- Three stops involve walking: the Rasohe quarry (about 2 km of trail from Splitska), the Dragon's Cave (a guided climb from Murvica) and the Blaca Hermitage (a footpath, or a boat from Bol).

- Photo tasks never ask anyone to climb walls, enter private courtyards or photograph people without asking.

- Water, a helmet and lights are mandatory. In summer, avoid riding in the midday heat.

## Game 1: Stone for an Emperor

**Sutivan → Supetar → Splitska (Rasohe quarry) → Postira → Lovrečina.** About 25 km by bike, plus 4 km on foot to Rasohe and back. It takes half a day and mostly follows the north coast, with a few climbs.

**Theme:** stone. Around the year 300, Emperor Diocletian builds his palace in Split, and Brač supplies the stone. The player follows that stone through 1,700 years and learns that on Brač, everything is built on top of something older.

**Opening letter** (handed out on the ferry or at the start):

> *If you are reading this, someone has given you my letters. Good. I don't have much time, and I have a lot to tell. I am a stonemason. I have worked for emperors, princes, monks and peasants, and I gave them all the same stone. I have left you five letters along the north coast. Each one is hidden where the stone still remembers. Collect five letters of the alphabet and you will have the first word. — R.*

### Stop 1: Sutivan, Kavanjin's summer house

> *Before there was a single house here, there was a villa. One of the emperor's men built it facing Split, while across the channel his master raised a palace. Fourteen hundred years later, on the same spot, a nobleman from Split built a summer house. His name was Jerolim Kavanjin. Here he wrote a poem of 32,724 lines, and he argued with his neighbours even more than he wrote. I carried stone for the emperor's man and for the poet. Remember the first rule of this island: here, we always build on something older.*

**History:** According to local sources, the summer house was built between 1690 and 1705, and the remains of a Roman villa are thought to lie beneath its garden. Kavanjin wrote the longest work of early Croatian literature here, known today as *Wealth and Poverty*. Its original title is *The Gospel Story of the Rich but Unhappy Epulun and the Poor but Honest Lazarus*. The name Sutivan comes from *Sveti Ivan*, Saint John.

**Photo task:** The summer house photographed from the waterfront, with the stone façade and the sea both in the frame.

**Letter:** Kavanjin's poem tells the story of a rich man and the poor Lazarus. What is the rich man called in the poem's original title? Take the first letter. → **E** (Epulun)

**True or legend?** *The people of Sutivan have a cat in their bell tower.*

**Clue:** *Ride east, to the town named after a fisherman.*

### Stop 2: Supetar, the cemetery on St Nicholas peninsula

> *This town is named after Saint Peter, the fisherman. Its first church had a mosaic floor, and a piece of that floor has survived fifteen centuries. But go to the cemetery. You will see the white dome you saw from the ferry. A young man once crossed the ocean as a stowaway, with nothing, and came back rich enough to build a tomb this island had never seen. The finest sculptor in Supetar was meant to carve it. The job was taken from him. Stone remembers glory. It also remembers injustice.*

**History:** The Petrinović family mausoleum was built in 1924–1927 from white Brač stone. It was designed by the sculptor Toma Rosandić as a cross-shaped building with five domes. The commission first went to Ivan Rendić, who considered it his life's work, but it was taken away from him. Frano Petrinović went to Chile as a stowaway and became a shipowner and mine owner there. The early Christian basilica of St Peter is the only one on Brač with part of its floor mosaic preserved.

**Photo task:** The mausoleum with the sea behind it. This is a cemetery: photograph quietly and respectfully.

**Letter:** What was the first name of the sculptor who designed the mausoleum? → **T** (Toma)

**True or legend?** *The mausoleum cost a million dollars.*

**Clue:** *Keep riding east, to a small harbour whose name sounds like the city across the channel. Above it, in the rock, a giant is waiting.*

### Stop 3: Splitska and the Rasohe quarry

> *The giant is not alive, but he is older than every house on the island. We quarrymen carved him into the living rock at the entrance to the quarry, so he would protect us while we worked. We made offerings to him on a stone altar and prayed that no block would crush our feet. Look at his hands. We made them too big on purpose. Anyone who cuts stone knows that strength lives in the hands. From these pits, stone travelled down to the harbour, and from the harbour across the sea, into the emperor's palace.*

**History:** A themed trail called *Herkules*, about 2 km long, leads from the Splitska waterfront to the quarry. The relief is about 80 cm tall. Hercules wears the skin of the Nemean lion over his shoulder, holds the apples of the Hesperides in one hand and a club in the other. His beard dates the carving to the 3rd century. Three Roman quarries lie between Splitska and Škrip: Plate, Stražišće and Rasohe. The village also has a 16th-century castle of the Cerinić family.

**Photo task:** The whole figure of Hercules, with both oversized hands clearly visible. Do not touch the relief.

**Letter:** Hercules wears the skin of a lion he defeated. Which lion? Take the first letter. → **N** (Nemean)

**True or legend?** *The stone for Diocletian's Palace was quarried right here.*

**Clue:** *Go back to the coast road and ride to the village whose name means pasture.*

### Stop 4: Postira

> *In 1876 a boy was born in this village who could see me. I don't know how. He was small and sickly, and he looked at stone as if he were reading it. When he grew up, he wrote a book about a shepherd who lives on Brač for centuries and never dies. He changed my name and my trade. But I know he was writing about me.*

**History:** The name Postira probably comes from the Latin *pastura*, pasture. Vladimir Nazor (1876–1949), one of the great Croatian writers, was born here, and his birth house is a protected monument. Nazor wrote the novel *Pastir Loda* (Loda the Shepherd), about a being who lives on Brač for thousands of years. The parish church of St John the Baptist dates from the mid-16th century.

**Photo task:** Nazor's birth house from the outside, or the waterfront with the church of St John in the frame.

**Letter:** In Nazor's book, what is the name of the immortal shepherd? Take the second letter. → **O** (Loda)

**True or legend?** *Nazor's famous giant, Veli Jože, lived on Brač.*

**Clue:** *East of Postira, behind the headland, lies a sandy beach where a church once burned.*

### Stop 5: Lovrečina Bay

> *You will rarely find sand on this island. Here there is, and beside it lie the walls of a great church. People were baptised here while Rome still remembered its emperors. Then came those from the sea who do not build, but burn. You have your first word. Now look inland, towards the hill above Splitska. There, in a village of sharp stones, two imperial women are sleeping. You will reach them the long way, across the whole west of the island. The second letter is waiting for you in Supetar.*

**History:** The ruins of the basilica of St Lawrence date from the 5th–6th centuries. The complex had a baptistery and a Benedictine monastery, and it is mentioned in the Povlja Charter of 1250. It was probably burned by pirates in the Middle Ages. Pilgrims still come here on St Lawrence's Day, 10 August.

**Photo task:** The ruins of the basilica with the sea behind them.

**Letter:** What makes this bay so unusual on Brač? Take the first letter. → **S** (sand)

**True or legend?** *The basilica was burned by pirates.*

**Secret word:** Unscramble E, T, N, O, S into **STONE**. It unlocks Game 2, which starts in Supetar and ends in Škrip.

## Game 2: The Prince's Seal

**Supetar → Bobovišća and Bobovišća na Moru → Ložišća → Milna → Dračevica → Nerežišća → Škrip.** About 60 km with a lot of climbing, because the interior lies at 300–400 m. E-bikes are recommended for a one-day ride. The route can also be split into two half-days, with a break in Milna.

**Theme:** power and memory. For about eight centuries, Brač was governed from Nerežišća. When the seat of government moved to Supetar in 1827, in our story the island's seal disappeared. The player searches for it from the newest centre to the oldest, and discovers that the seal is not an object.

**Opening letter:**

> *When they moved the prince's seat from Nerežišća to Supetar in 1827, they moved the books, the keys and the flag. They never found the seal. They asked me, because I had carved it. I said I didn't know. I lied. Ride west, then into the heart of the island. I will show you where it is. But be warned: whoever looks for it in a drawer will never find it. — R.*

### Stop 1: Supetar, the grave of Ivan Rendić

> *This is the island's new centre, and here lies a man who understood stone better than any of us. He died poor, and he left two hundred statues behind. The mausoleum at the edge of this cemetery should have been his, and it wasn't. But one bell tower in a village to the west is his, the very one nobody believed would ever be built. There you will learn what island stubbornness means.*

**History:** Supetar has officially been the island's centre since 1827. Ivan Rendić (1849–1932) is considered the first great Croatian sculptor of the modern age and made about 200 sculptures. He grew up on Brač and lived modestly all his life. Several of his works stand in the Supetar cemetery.

**Photo task:** Rendić's grave or one of his sculptures in the cemetery. Photograph quietly and respectfully.

**Letter:** What is the surname of the sculptor who lost the commission for the Petrinović mausoleum? → **R** (Rendić)

**True or legend?** *Supetar has always been the capital of Brač.*

**Clue:** *Ride west, to a bay where witches dance and warriors sleep with their helmets on.*

### Stop 2: Bobovišća na Moru and Vičja Luka

> *This bay splits in two. One arm holds fishermen and sailing boats; the other holds ancient graves. Warriors lay in them with Corinthian helmets, buried two hundred years before anyone had heard of Rome. The old people say that at night witches gather in Vičja Luka. They also say that a farmer on Vidova gora once lost a pair of oxen down a pit, and a few days later their yoke floated up here, on the other side of the island. The island is hollow, my friend. Beneath your wheels runs water nobody has ever seen. Remember that pit. We will come back for it.*

**History:** Graves above Vičja Luka have yielded finds from the 5th and 4th centuries BC: coins, bronze helmets and greaves made in Corinthian workshops. The bay is dominated by the fortified castle of the Gligo family, once a warehouse for grain, oil and wine. Stone mooring rings for ships are still set in its walls. Bobovišća na Moru is the only settlement on Brač without its own church. Vladimir Nazor had a family house here and went to school in Bobovišća. Above the beach stand three columns called *The Three Sisters*, which Nazor raised in 1937 in honour of his sisters.

**Photo task:** A stone mooring ring on the wall of the Gligo castle, with the sea in the frame.

**Letter:** What is the monument of three columns above the bay called in English? Take the first letter. → **T** (The Three Sisters)

**True or legend?** *Bobovišća na Moru has no church.*

**Clue:** *Climb back up to the village once called the Big Village. Its bell can be heard all the way to the island of Šolta.*

### Stop 3: Ložišća, Rendić's bell tower

> *When the people of Ložišća asked for a bell tower design, the sculptor brought a bag full of drawings. He showed them two modest village towers and one magnificent one, just as a joke. "You can't build this one and you can't pay for it. This one is for Venice," he told them. And they said: "This one and no other!" They borrowed the money and paid the debt back in wine and olive oil. I was among those who carried the stone. No seal holds a village together like shared stubbornness.*

**History:** The bell tower of the parish church of Sts John and Paul was built in 1889 to a design by Ivan Rendić. Rendić reportedly wanted to repay Dr Mihovil Nazor of Ložišća, who had helped him study in Venice. The story of "the tower for Venice" was written down in the memoirs of Frane Pavlov, the village barber and postman. Ložišća is one of the best-preserved traditional villages on the island.

**Photo task:** The bell tower from the foot of the stone steps, with the whole tower in the frame, top to bottom.

**Letter:** In which country did Rendić study art, with help from a doctor from Ložišća? Take the first letter. → **I** (Italy)

**True or legend?** *Rendić originally designed this bell tower for Venice.*

**Clue:** *Ride down to the west, to the harbour where the ships of two emperors fought one December night.*

### Stop 4: Milna and the Baterija fort

> *In December 1806 the French set up cannons on the headland above the harbour, to keep the Russian fleet out of the islands. The fort was built by 150 of our peasants. One night, French ships set out from Split to surprise a Russian ship anchored off Milna. But someone lit fires on the shore, and it was the French who were surprised. Who lit the fires? The island, my friend. The island never liked it when someone else stamped their seal on it.*

**History:** Milna was settled in the 16th century by shepherds from Nerežišća, and from the 17th to the 19th century it was the busiest port on Brač. The French built the Baterija fort on Cape Zaglav in 1806. In December of that year the French spotted a Russian reconnaissance brig, the *Aleksandar*, commanded by Ivan Skolavski, near Milna. General Marmont sent a small flotilla of gunboats against it. The night battle lasted three hours: one French gunboat sank, and the damaged ship *Napoleon* later sank in Split harbour. According to the Milna tourist board, islanders unhappy with French rule lit five signal fires to tell the Russians the size of the French fleet. The Russians left Brač in 1807, after the Treaty of Tilsit.

**Photo task:** Milna harbour with the church of Our Lady of the Annunciation in the frame, taken from the waterfront.

**Letter:** What was the name of the Russian reconnaissance brig near Milna? → **A** (Aleksandar)

**True or legend?** *Islanders used fires to betray the French positions to the Russians.*

**Clue:** *Climb inland, to a village founded by refugees, which once ground flour for the whole island.*

### Stop 5: Dračevica

> *Refugees from Poljica came here in 1574. They had nothing but their hands. They cleared stone, mound after mound, until they found the soil the Romans had found before them. On the hill they dug out a Roman cistern and filled it with water again. Their priest wrote down the village's history, and one of their descendants became a scholar who read the names of every place on Brač. Princes write laws. Ordinary people write the island.*

**History:** Dračevica was founded by refugees from Poljica, on the mainland, who settled on Brač under a Venetian decree of 31 December 1574. They built the church of Sts Cosmas and Damian in 1678. The walls of the largest windmill on Brač stand above the village. Parish priest Nikola Šimunović wrote a handwritten history of Dračevica in 1775. The linguist and academician Petar Šimunović, author of a major study of Brač place names, was born here.

**Photo task:** The walls of the old windmill on the hilltop above the village, or a dry-stone wall with an olive tree.

**Letter:** Petar Šimunović was a member of the Croatian Academy of Sciences and Arts. What is a member of an academy called? → **A** (academician)

**True or legend?** *Dračevica had the largest windmill on Brač.*

**Clue:** *Ride to the island's crossroads, to the town with three squares that no longer has a prince.*

### Stop 6: Nerežišća

> *For eight centuries the seal was stamped here. The prince's palace stood on the highest square. It is gone now, but the square remains, and people still gather in it. Not far from here, in a small church, Saint George is slaying a dragon. Remember that dragon. It is not the only one on the island. The real one is waiting for you in the south, in the rock.*

**History:** Nerežišća was the island's administrative centre and the seat of the Prince of Brač from roughly the year 1000 until the Napoleonic era. It lies inland because of the fear of pirates. The town has three squares; the prince's palace and the parish church of Our Lady of Mount Carmel stood on the highest. The small church of St George near Nerežišća reportedly shows the saint fighting a dragon; this should be checked on site before publication.

**Photo task:** The parish church of Our Lady of Mount Carmel from the highest square.

**Letter:** How many squares does Nerežišća have? Take the first letter of the number. → **T** (three)

**True or legend?** *Nerežišća was the capital of the island for eight centuries.*

**Clue:** *Ride north, to the oldest village on the island, where I have been waiting for you from the start.*

### Stop 7: Škrip

> *This place is older than any seal. The Illyrians stacked these walls from enormous blocks of stone, without mortar. The Romans built a tomb on top of them, and someone later built a tower on top of the tomb. The old people say that Prisca and Valeria, the wife and daughter of Emperor Diocletian, lie in that tomb, exiled from Split by the emperor for becoming Christians. Now you know where the seal is. It is not in a drawer. It is pressed into everything you saw today: into stone, into walls, into the island's name. The Romans called this island Brattia.*
>
> *But the story is not over. Remember the pit on Vidova gora and the yoke that floated up in Vičja Luka. The third letter is waiting for you at the summit, where an old god with four faces once watched over all four corners of the world.*

**History:** Škrip is the oldest settlement on Brač. Part of the Illyrian walls can be seen in the courtyard of the Brač Island Museum, housed in the Radojković tower, which stands on the foundations of a Roman mausoleum. The name probably comes from the Latin *scrupus*, sharp stone. Another legend says St Helena, mother of Emperor Constantine, was born in Škrip. The village also has a 16th-century castle of the Cerinić family and Roman sarcophagi.

**Photo task:** The Illyrian walls in the museum courtyard, with the tower in the frame.

**Letter:** The Illyrian walls are built without mortar from huge stone \_\_\_. Take the first letter. → **B** (blocks)

**True or legend?** *Diocletian's wife and daughter are buried in Škrip.*

**Secret word:** Unscramble R, T, I, A, A, T, B into **BRATTIA**, the Roman name of the island.

## Game 3: The Dragon's Eye

**Nerežišća → Vidova gora → Bol (Zlatni Rat, Dominican monastery) → Murvica and the Dragon's Cave → Blaca Hermitage.** About 40 km by bike. From Nerežišća to the summit the climb is about 400 m, followed by a long, steep descent to Bol, so check your brakes. The Dragon's Cave and Blaca require walking, so this is a full "bike + boots" day. Blaca can also be reached on a boat trip from Bol, so the game can be spread over two days.

**Theme:** seeing. On this side of the island, people spent a thousand years looking: at the sky, into the rock, towards God and up at the stars. The player finds out what the dragon was guarding.

**Opening letter** (read at the start in Nerežišća):

> *You have seen Saint George slaying a dragon. That dragon is painted, which makes it easy to kill. The real one is carved into the rock, and nobody has ever killed it. But first you must climb as high as this island allows and look. Don't look at the sea. Look at the island. — R.*

### Stop 1: Vidova gora

> *When the first Croats came here, they say they dedicated this summit to a god with four faces, who looked towards all four corners of the world. They called him Svetovid, the one who sees everything. Later a small church to Saint Vitus was built on the summit. Do you know who Saint Vitus was? A young man put to death by the same emperor whose wife, they say, is buried in Škrip. On this island, everything is connected. In the forest below the summit there is a pit the old people call Vičja. Don't go in. Just remember that it and the bay at Bobovišća breathe the same air.*

**History:** Vidova gora (778 m) is the highest peak of all the Adriatic islands. It takes its name from a small 13th- or 14th-century church of St Vitus (Sveti Vid), whose ruins lie about 80 m west of the viewpoint. The Svetovid theory comes from place-name research by Petar Šimunović, but there is no material evidence for it. St Vitus was martyred in Emperor Diocletian's persecutions in the early 4th century. Legend links the Vičja pit to Vičja Luka near Bobovišća.

**Photo task:** The classic view down onto Zlatni Rat. Second shot: the foundations of the church of St Vitus, which almost nobody photographs.

**Letter:** According to the theory, which old Slavic god was the summit dedicated to? → **S** (Svetovid)

**True or legend?** *Vidova gora is named after the old Slavic god Svetovid.*

**Clue:** *Ride down to the golden tongue that moves when nobody is watching.*

### Stop 2: Zlatni Rat

> *You looked at it from above. Now stand on its tip. This headland is never the same. Wind and sea push it around, so sometimes its tip points west and sometimes east. I call it the dragon's tail. When the tail moves, the dragon is awake.*

**History:** Zlatni Rat ("Golden Horn") is a pebble spit that changes shape with the winds and sea currents. It is especially famous for the summer north-westerly wind, the maestral, which draws windsurfers from all over Europe.

**Photo task:** From the tip of the spit, looking back up at Vidova gora. It is the reverse of the shot from the summit, so the player can compare the two photos.

**Letter:** What is the local name of the summer north-westerly wind that fills the sails here? Take the second letter. → **A** (maestral)

**True or legend?** *The tip of Zlatni Rat changes direction.*

**Clue:** *At the other end of Bol, on a small headland, monks keep a document signed by a prince.*

### Stop 3: The Dominican monastery in Bol

> *Remember the seal? Here is a real one. In 1475 the people of Bol gave the monks land on this headland, and the Prince of Brač confirmed it in a charter that still survives. The monks collected books, old coins and paintings. One painting is attributed to a great Venetian master. I don't know if it's his. I know these people knew how to keep things safe. And whoever knows how to keep things safe knows what a dragon guards.*

**History:** The monastery of St Mary of Mercy was founded in 1475. The original deed from the Prince of Brač, granting the monks their land, has survived. The monastery houses a museum, archaeological finds and a library of rare books and manuscripts. The altarpiece of the Madonna and Child is attributed to Tintoretto. Between the two world wars the monastery ran a classical grammar school.

**Photo task:** The monastery and church with the sea, photographed from the Bol waterfront.

**Letter:** To which Venetian master is the altarpiece attributed? → **T** (Tintoretto)

**True or legend?** *The painting in the monastery church is by Tintoretto.*

**Clue:** *Ride west to the village of Murvica. Above it, half an hour's climb, behind iron gates, the dragon sleeps.*

### Stop 4: The Dragon's Cave (Drakonjina špilja) above Murvica

> *In the 15th century, priests who prayed in their own language and wrote in their own alphabet took refuge here. They lived in the rock like swallows. Into the stone they carved Our Lady with angels, the moon, people, birds in their nests, and a dragon. Some say certain signs are older than them, left by people who prayed here to other gods. Look at the dragon. It doesn't look like it's guarding gold, does it? It looks like it's watching. The dragon is not the keeper of treasure. It is the keeper of the gaze.*

**History:** The cave lies about 180 m above Murvica. It is some 20 metres long and divided into four rooms. In the 15th century it was the home and chapel of Glagolitic priests from Poljica (priests who used the old Croatian Glagolitic script). Alongside the dragon, the moon, people and birds in nests are carved into the rock, and some experts see traces of pagan symbolism in them. Because of damage and theft the entrance is locked. Visits are possible only with a local guide who has the key, and the climb from Murvica takes 30–60 minutes.

**Photo task:** The dragon relief, if the guide allows photography. If not, the view from the cave towards the island of Hvar.

**Letter:** Beside Our Lady, winged figures are carved into the rock. What are they? → **A** (angels)

**True or legend?** *The dragon was carved by a monk in the 15th century.*

**Clue:** *The same people who lived with the dragon later moved down into a hidden valley. There, they looked even further: at the stars.*

### Stop 5: Blaca Hermitage

> *In 1551 two priests fleeing the Ottomans hid in a cave in this valley. They stayed for four hundred years. They cleared the forest, planted olives, printed books and taught children. The last of them, Father Niko, bought the biggest telescope in Croatia in Vienna and spent his nights watching the sky. He died in 1963, and the hermitage died with him. Today two minor planets carry his name and the name of this island. So now you know: the dragon was guarding the gaze, and here the gaze went all the way to the stars.*
>
> *Their library holds manuscripts in the old script once used in Poljica. In that same script, on a stone above the door of a church in the east of the island, my name is written. When you are ready, go to Povlja.*

**History:** The hermitage was founded in 1551 by Glagolitic priests from Poljica, in the Ljubitovica cave. It was a monastery, farm, school, printing house and observatory. Its last head, Father Nikola Miličević the Younger, bought a 178 mm refracting telescope in Vienna in 1926, then the largest telescope on Croatian soil. The hermitage ended with his death in 1963. Two asteroids discovered at the Višnjan Observatory were named Nikola Milicevic and Brac. Blaca has been on UNESCO's Tentative List of World Heritage since 2007.

**Photo task:** The hermitage built into the cliff, photographed from the path above it.

**Letter:** The largest telescope at Blaca gathered light with a lens, not a mirror. What is this kind of telescope called? → **R** (refractor)

**True or legend?** *Two asteroids are named Brač and Nikola Miličević.*

**Secret word:** Unscramble S, A, T, A, R into **ASTRA**, Latin for "stars". Keep it safe: you will need it for the finale of the big game.

## Game 4: The Shepherd Who Remembers

**Five days by bike, in a loop around the whole island: Supetar → west → the heart of the island → south → east → north → Škrip.** The game joins all three short games and adds the east of the island, where the player learns who R. is. Without day 3 (the walking stops) it can be shortened to four days.

**Theme:** memory. So far the player has collected words. Now they discover who left them, and why.

**Opening letter** (on the ferry to Supetar):

> *This time you won't get five letters, or seven. You will get the whole island. You will go everywhere I have worked, and at the end you will learn my name. Don't fear the climbs. The people who lived here carried stone on their backs up these same hills. All you have is a bicycle. — R.*

### Day-by-day plan

All distances are approximate and must be checked on a map.

| Day                  | Route                                                                                  | Approx.          | Stops                                 |
|----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------|---------------------------------------|
| 1\. Seal and sea     | Supetar → Sutivan → Bobovišća → Ložišća → Milna                                        | 35 km            | From Games 1 and 2                    |
| 2\. Heart and summit | Milna → Dračevica → Nerežišća → Donji Humac (Kopačina) → Nerežišća → Vidova gora → Bol | 50 km, big climb | From Games 2 and 3, **new:** Kopačina |
| 3\. Dragon and stars | Zlatni Rat, Dominicans, Murvica and the Dragon's Cave, Blaca (by boat from Bol)        | 15 km + walking  | From Game 3                           |
| 4\. The east         | Bol → Gornji Humac → Selca → Sumartin → Povlja                                         | 45 km            | **New:** Selca, Sumartin, Povlja      |
| 5\. The return       | Povlja → Pučišća → Lovrečina → Postira → Splitska → Škrip → Supetar                    | 45 km            | **New:** Pučišća, finale in Škrip     |

Stops from the short games use the same letters and photo tasks. Players who have already played them get one new task instead: photograph a detail they missed the first time. The final password is built like this:

- **ASTRA** comes from the five Game 3 stops: Vidova gora, Zlatni Rat, the Dominicans, the Dragon's Cave and Blaca.

- **ASPERA** comes from six stops: Dračevica (new question), Kopačina, Selca, Sumartin, Pučišća and Škrip.

- **PER ... AD ...** is the frame Radonja gives the player in Povlja.

### Stop: Dračevica, second visit (day 2)

> *You have been here before, but you never asked what the village's name means. When the refugees from Poljica arrived on this land, they were met by stone and thorns. They spent years clearing them. The village's name, they say, comes from those thorns. Remember them. At the end of the road you will need a word that means thorns, and it won't be Croatian.*

**History:** The name Dračevica most likely comes from *drača*, a thorny shrub common on the karst. Dračeva Luka near Murvica, where the Glagolitic hermits lived, shares the same root. The exact etymology should be checked in *Bračka toponimija* (Brač Place Names) by Petar Šimunović, who was himself born in Dračevica.

**Photo task:** A thorny shrub beside a dry-stone wall at the edge of the village.

**Letter:** The village is probably named after a thorny shrub. What is it called in Croatian? Take the last letter. → **A** (drača)

**True or legend?** *Dračevica is named after a thorny shrub.*

**Clue:** *Ride towards Nerežišća and on to Donji Humac. There, past the cemetery, the oldest home on the island is waiting.*

### Stop: Kopačina Cave near Donji Humac (day 2)

> *Before the emperors, before the Illyrians, before any walls, people lived here who knew nothing of houses or writing. They hunted wild horses, wild cattle and deer, and they made their knives and arrowheads from stone. Much later the Greeks named this island after its deer. These people knew why. This is where I picked up my first stone. It was not for a wall or a statue. It was for staying alive. Everything you will see on this island began here, stone by stone.*

**History:** Kopačina is the most important prehistoric site on the island. It is reached on foot along a gravel track north-west of Donji Humac, about 20 minutes' walk; the track turns off near the cemetery. The cave is 12 metres long and has two chambers. It holds the oldest traces of people on Brač: bones of wild horse, wild cattle and deer, along with stone knives, drills and an arrowhead. The entrance is closed with a grille because of ongoing excavations. Some finds are on display in the Brač Island Museum in Škrip. The island's Greek name, Elaphousa, probably comes from *elaphos*, meaning deer.

**Photo task:** The view from the small plateau in front of the cave, down the valley towards Bobovišća. Do not enter the cave.

**Letter:** What did these hunters make their knives and arrowheads from? → **S** (stone)

**True or legend?** *People lived in Kopačina more than 10,000 years ago.*

**Clue:** *Go back to Nerežišća and climb towards the top of the island.*

### Stop: Selca, Tolstoy Park

> *In 1911 this village decided to raise a monument to a man none of them had ever seen: a writer from the far side of Europe who had died the year before. Half the village wanted someone else, a local bishop. They argued in the council. In the end, students of the stonemasonry school, guided by a Czech sculptor, carved the face of the Russian writer. In his homeland, a committee for his monument was only just being formed. Selca got there first. Stone remembers what we decide to remember. Keep that in mind. You'll need it.*

**History:** The idea was launched in 1911 by the Slovak writer Matej Bencúr, known by his pen name Martin Kukučín, who was working in Selca as the village doctor. He was an enthusiastic supporter of Pan-Slavism, the movement for the brotherhood of Slavic peoples, and a great admirer of Russian literature. After a debate between supporters of Tolstoy and of Bishop Strossmayer, Tolstoy won. The bust was made in 1914 by students of the Selca stonemasonry school, from a model by the Czech sculptor Jaroslav Barda. Selca also has many other statues and busts, and the monumental church of Christ the King.

**Photo task:** Tolstoy's bust in the park. Second shot: any other monument in the village, so the player sees how many there are.

**Letter:** What was the name of the movement for the brotherhood of Slavic peoples that inspired the village doctor of Selca? → **P** (Pan-Slavism)

**True or legend?** *Selca raised the world's first monument to Tolstoy.*

**Clue:** *Ride down to the harbour where refugees from the mainland, led by monks in brown habits, founded a village.*

### Stop: Sumartin

> *The people of Poljica fled to Dračevica. The Glagolitic priests fled to Blaca. And these people fled too, across the channel from Makarska, from the same shadow in the east. Franciscan friars and the families who followed them founded this place in the mid-17th century. Have you noticed? For centuries this island has taken in those who run. That is why it is so hard on the outside and so gentle within.*

**History:** Franciscan friars and refugees from the Makarska coast, fleeing Ottoman raids, arrived here by boat and founded Sumartin on 11 November 1646, St Martin's Day, which gave the village its name. The poet and friar Andrija Kačić Miošić lived in Sumartin from 1745 to 1750 and had the monastery built in its present form; an inscription at the entrance to the monastery building says so. The monastery keeps Baroque art, archives written in Croatian Cyrillic and a large library.

**Photo task:** The Franciscan monastery with the harbour, and the Biokovo mountains across the channel behind it.

**Letter:** The inscription at the monastery entrance names the friar-poet who had it built. What was his first name? → **A** (Andrija)

**True or legend?** *Sumartin was founded by refugees from the mainland.*

**Clue:** *Ride north, to the cove where my name is carved above the door of an old church.*

### Stop: Povlja, the revelation

> *Here we are. Look at the stone above the door. Around 1184, an abbot had a doorway cut into the wall of the old baptistery, and above it an inscription was carved in old Croatian letters. It names Prince Brečko and a stonemason: Master Radonja.*
>
> *That's me.*
>
> *Don't ask how I lived so many centuries. Maybe I didn't. Maybe Radonja was every quarryman at Rasohe, every farmer in Dračevica, every mason who carried stone for Rendić's bell tower. But this is where my name was first written down, so here I will not lie.*
>
> *Sixty-six years after the inscription, a scribe copied onto parchment all the lands of this monastery: fields, hollows, coves, churches, even the one on the sand at Lovrečina. It is the oldest record of this island. They say that centuries later a housewife nearly used it to light her fire. That is how little it takes for an island to forget itself.*

**History:** The Povlja lintel was carved shortly after 1184 above the door of the Benedictine abbey of St John the Baptist in Povlja. Alongside Prince Brečko, it names the stonemason Master Radonja. The Povlja Charter was written on 1 December 1250 in Croatian Cyrillic script (bosančica) by the notary Ivan, a canon of Split cathedral. It is the oldest document written in Croatian Cyrillic and the oldest surviving document from Brač; the linguist Petar Šimunović called it "the birth certificate of the island of Brač". The story of its accidental discovery beside a hearth circulates in popular sources and should be checked with the parish.

**Photo task:** The early Christian baptistery and church from the outside. If the lintel is accessible and photography is allowed, photograph the inscription.

**Letter:** There is no letter here. Instead, Radonja gives you the frame of the final sentence, in Latin, the language of the Church and of learned people in his time: ***PER ... AD ...*** You will earn the two missing words yourself.

**True or legend?** *The Povlja Charter was found just as a housewife was about to use it to light a fire.*

**Clue:** *Tomorrow, ride along the north coast to the town of white stone where young people still learn my trade.*

### Stop: Pučišća, the stonemasonry school

> *You wonder if I am immortal. I'm not, my friend. Nobody is. But every autumn, young men and women come to this school, pick up a chisel and a mallet, and strike stone for the first time. I heard that same sound at Rasohe eighteen centuries ago. As long as someone here is carving, I am alive.*

**History:** Pučišća is known for its stone-working tradition and its stonemasonry school, where students still learn to shape Brač stone by hand. On Brač, a master stonemason is called a *meštar*. The school's founding date and visiting conditions should be checked directly with the school.

**Photo task:** Pučišća harbour, surrounded by white stone houses. If the school allows visits, the stonemasons' workshop too.

**Letter:** What do people on Brač call a master stonemason? Take the second letter. → **E** (meštar)

**True or legend?** *Brač stone was used in the White House in Washington.*

**Clue:** *Ride west past Lovrečina, Postira and Splitska. Climb to where it all began.*

### Finale: Škrip

> *You have come back to the oldest walls on the island. Before the Roman emperors, before the princes and monks, people here laid stone upon stone, without mortar. The walls still stand.*
>
> *Now take the frame I gave you in Povlja and fill in what you have earned. You came through the thorns of Dračevica, the stone of Rasohe and the steep slopes of Vidova gora. You reached the stars Father Niko watched from Blaca.*
>
> *PER ASPERA AD ASTRA. Through hardship to the stars.*
>
> *That is how this island was made, and that is how you have travelled it. Don't carve anything into the stone; there's no need. Tell someone what you have seen, and come back. — Radonja*

**History:** Together with the Brač Island Museum, this is the place with the most layers on the island: Illyrian walls, a Roman mausoleum, a medieval tower and a Renaissance castle. The Brač Island Museum is housed in the tower of the Radojković family and holds finds from every period the player has read about.

**Photo task:** A group photo of the players beside the Illyrian walls. This is the player's "signature" in the chronicle.

**Letter:** Which family's tower houses the Brač Island Museum today? → **R** (Radojković)

**True or legend?** *St Helena, mother of Emperor Constantine, was born in Škrip.*

**Secret word:** Unscramble A, S, P, A, E, R from Dračevica, Kopačina, Selca, Sumartin, Pučišća and Škrip into **ASPERA**. Write it into the frame from Povlja together with ASTRA from Game 3. The final password is **PER ASPERA AD ASTRA**.

To finish, players ride downhill to Supetar (6–8 km) for the ferry.

### Fact and fiction

- **Invented for the game:** R. as an immortal stonemason, the seal that vanished in 1827, the idea that Nazor "saw" R., and R. working in every era. All of this is the story frame.

- **Real:** Master Radonja existed, because he is named on the Povlja lintel. Nothing else is known about him.

- **Real local legends:** Prisca and Valeria in Škrip, St Helena, the Vičja pit and Vičja Luka, the witches of Bobovišća, and Svetovid on Vidova gora.

- **Scripts:** the Povlja lintel and charter are written in Croatian Cyrillic (bosančica), not Glagolitic. The hermit-priests of the Dragon's Cave and Blaca were Glagolitic. Game cards could print the letters from Games 1–3 in Glagolitic, with a key to Croatian Cyrillic handed out in Povlja for Game 4.
